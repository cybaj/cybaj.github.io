Physics was in good order around 1900. Newtonian mechanics got the planets
right, Maxwell's equations had shown light to be an electromagnetic wave, and
statistical mechanics accounted for the behaviour of gases. Only a handful of
problems seemed to remain. Those few resisted classical physics, and a new
mechanics was needed to account for them.

## Five disagreements

Set the classical prediction beside what the experiment actually showed.
(personal note: 1장 양자역학의 기원)

| Classical prediction | What was observed |
|---|---|
| A hot body should radiate unboundedly at short wavelengths | Intensity peaks at some wavelength and falls to zero below it |
| An atom may hold any energy, so its spectrum should be continuous | Only lines at particular wavelengths appear |
| If light is a wave, brighter light should eject faster electrons | The electron's kinetic energy is set by frequency, not intensity |
| An electron is a particle, so it should follow a definite path | Electrons diffract and interfere |
| Position and velocity should both be knowable exactly at once | The two cannot be narrowed together |

Five apparently separate experiments, telling one story: energy and angular
momentum are not continuous quantities, and neither matter nor light is one of
particle-or-wave.

## The pages

**What experiment demanded**

- **[The quantum hypothesis]({{< ref "origins.md" >}})** — blackbody radiation,
  the failure of Rayleigh–Jeans, the Planck distribution, the photoelectric
  effect, de Broglie, uncertainty. Where classical physics broke.

**The replacement**

- [The wavefunction and the Schrödinger equation]({{< ref "wavefunction.md" >}}) —
  giving up the trajectory to gain a wavefunction. The eigenvalue problem, the
  Born interpretation, normalisation, and the four conditions a wavefunction
  must satisfy.

**Three problems that solve** — three ways a boundary condition makes
quantisation.

- **[Particle in a box]({{< ref "particle-in-a-box.md" >}})** — trapped between
  walls. Zero-point energy, degeneracy, the correspondence principle, and the
  colour of a dye.
- **[The harmonic oscillator]({{< ref "oscillator.md" >}})** — bound to a
  spring. Why every potential is a parabola near its minimum, and why bonds
  vibrate even at absolute zero.
- **[Rotation and angular momentum]({{< ref "rotation.md" >}})** — coming back
  around. Quantisation without walls, and where the shapes of orbitals are
  decided.

**Atoms**

- **[The hydrogenic atom]({{< ref "hydrogen.md" >}})** — put in the Coulomb
  potential and the Rydberg formula comes out. Three quantum numbers and the
  orbitals.
- **[Many-electron atoms]({{< ref "many-electron.md" >}})** — two electrons
  already defeat exact solution. The orbital approximation, the Pauli
  principle, shielding and penetration, the periodic table.
- **[Atomic spectra]({{< ref "atomic-spectra.md" >}})** — reading the levels
  off an experiment. Selection rules, singlets and triplets, fine structure.

**Molecules**

- **[The Born–Oppenheimer approximation]({{< ref "born-oppenheimer.md" >}})** —
  separating nuclei from electrons. The molecular potential energy surface.
- **[Valence-bond theory]({{< ref "valence-bond.md" >}})** — a bond as an
  overlap. Electron pairs, σ and π, resonance, hybridisation.
- **[Molecular orbital theory]({{< ref "molecular-orbital.md" >}})** — electrons
  belonging to the whole molecule. LCAO, bond order, and why oxygen sticks to a
  magnet.
