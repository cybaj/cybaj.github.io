# wiki — Usage

## Item-wide research

Use it first, at the `gathering` stage, on a topic you are still mapping. One
article gives you the vocabulary, the shape of the field, and the names of the
people and papers that matter.

Then leave. Read the References section, pick the primary sources it cites, and
harvest those with the `web` source. The wiki reference stays in `references/`
as the thing that pointed you there — not as the thing you cite.

For a bilingual item, harvest both language editions when both exist. The
Korean article tells you the Korean terminology, which is otherwise easy to get
subtly wrong.

## Per-post research

A definition, a date, a spelling, or the standard name for something you are
about to state in passing. Cheap to check, embarrassing to get wrong.

## Cautions

- **Cite what it cites.** A claim sourced to Wikipedia in a published post is a
  claim you did not check. Follow the footnote.
- **The language editions are different documents.** `기계 학습` and
  `Machine learning` are not translations of each other — different length,
  different sourcing, sometimes different claims. Citing the Korean article for
  something you actually read in English is a real error, and the separate
  reference files exist so you can tell which is which.
- **A missing counterpart means something.** If `langlinks` returns nothing,
  the topic may be framed differently in that language rather than absent.
  Search before concluding it does not exist.
- **Revisions move.** This is why `url` pins an `oldid`. If you re-check a
  claim later and the article now says otherwise, that is a finding worth
  noting, not a mistake to quietly correct.
- **Watch the talk page on anything contentious.** A stable-looking article can
  be the current truce rather than a settled account.
